# Territory Opportunity Brief scoping conversation

**Status:** DRAFT FOR HENRY TO USE. Fifteen minutes. No automated sending or calling.

## Before the conversation

Confirm the person is a named decision-maker at a recruitment, staffing, or supplier business selling into domiciliary care in Birmingham or the West Midlands. For a call, complete TPS and CTPS screening. Identify yourself and H-Kay Limited at the start.

## Minute 0 to 2: opening

"Hello, is that [name]? My name is Henry from H-Kay. I'll be brief. I'm speaking with firms that sell into the care sector. We use CQC's openly published data under the Open Government Licence, and we are independent of CQC. We turn the published record into a shortlist of which care organisations are worth reviewing in a territory, with a stated reason for each. Is finding the right accounts something your team spends time on?"

Do not say CareGist tracks data in real time, has complete data, predicts a rating, or knows that an organisation needs staff.

## Minute 2 to 7: discovery

Ask these three questions, then listen:

1. "How do you decide which care organisations to approach at the moment?"
2. "Which territory matters most to you over the next six months?"
3. "What would it change if you knew which providers had just registered or had a documented rating movement?"

For a recruitment buyer, listen for how new providers, verified group footprint, service mix, and territory coverage affect account planning. Do not infer a vacancy, staffing shortage, or expansion from a registration or shared provider ID.

## Minute 7 to 10: scope the decision

"Which decision would you want the brief to reduce: whether to enter a territory, which accounts to assign first, or where your team should spend the next quarter?"

Confirm:

- the target territory;
- the relevant care service types;
- who will use the dataset and shortlist;
- the current account-selection process;
- what evidence the buyer needs beside each priority account.

If the buyer asks for another price, scope, turnaround, customer count, performance figure, or handling of their own data, say: "That is outside what I can commit to. Let me check and come back to you today."

## Minute 10 to 13: offer the sample

"Let me send you a sample. It is a real piece of work covering domiciliary care in Birmingham and Solihull: the dataset, the shortlist with reasons, and the executive summary. The sample is built from a repository snapshot and is labelled not verified, so you can see both the work and its limits. If it is useful, the same Territory Opportunity Brief for your territory is £795 and takes three working days, once the delivery gates pass."

If they already use a database:

"A database tells you who exists. This brief identifies which accounts deserve review and states why. Take the sample and compare it with what you already have."

If they say £795 is a lot for a spreadsheet:

"The dataset is one of three parts. The other two are the shortlist with a reason against each account and the brief explaining how the territory is structured. Look at the sample before deciding."

If they say they can get it from CQC:

"You can. It is public data and we say so openly. The work is pulling out the relevant observations, deciding which changes matter to your business, and ranking them."

If they ask how many customers CareGist has:

"We're early. You would be among the first, which is why I am offering the sample rather than asking you to rely on a sales claim. I would rather you judged the work."

If they say not now:

"Understood. What would have to be different for it to be worth a look?"

Record the answer verbatim.

## Minute 13 to 15: mandatory close

"I will send the Birmingham and Solihull sample for you to review. Can we book 15 minutes on [date and time] so I can hear whether it would change an account or territory decision for you?"

The close requires both actions: offer the sample and book the callback. A scoping conversation comes before any payment ask. Do not include or promise a Stripe Payment Link at this stage.
