## Plain-English price defence

The defensible reason to charge £795 is the work of answering a specific account-selection question: which care organisations should this buyer review first, and what evidence supports each choice? The purchase should include a checked territory workbook, a shortlist the buyer can inspect and use, and a short explanation of the priorities and limitations.

The current sample only does part of that work. It gives the buyer an organised extract and a date-sorted list, then hands the relevance checking back to them. I would not defend £795 for the sample as it stands. Accurate rows and a professional document are necessary, but they are not enough to substantiate the promised decision support.

After the improvement below, the honest buyer explanation is: “The £795 covers a territory-specific account review: the dataset, 25–50 organisations selected against your agreed criteria, the evidence behind each selection, and a short brief explaining what to review next. You see the scope and a checked sample before deciding. We do not know yet how much time it will save your team, and we do not promise sales.” This wording describes an intended completed deliverable, not work already done.

£795 is the founder-selected price to test. It is not a market-validated valuation. The inspected records contain no paid acceptance of this Brief, measured buyer time saving, or verified revenue impact. The founder's approximately four-hour production estimate would imply £198.75 of fee per estimated production hour; that is arithmetic on an unmeasured estimate, not profit or buyer value. VAT and acquisition/support costs are not settled by that calculation.

The earlier £150 fairness paper [S10] does not close the value gap. It compares a manual digest with broader subscriptions and consultant rates, assumes 6–10 hours of effort, and links price to a hypothetical consultancy engagement. None establishes how a buyer uses the actual one-event sample. Its data refers to one hospital rating publication, not a demonstrated qualified sales opportunity. £150/4 = £37.50 per digest is correct; calling four weeks a calendar month is imprecise. No competitor-price or ROI claim from that paper is reused here, and no new price has been invented.

**Verified value:** organised source data, reproducible selection, explicit source references and territory counts. **Assumed value:** better account decisions, less research time, worthwhile buyer use and acceptance of £795. The next commercial test should ask the buyer to identify a concrete account decision the improved sample changes, then record an explicit willingness-to-pay response. Polite interest does not establish value or revenue.
